# ASSEMBLED ASSETS

> **DOMAIN:** ASSETS / RETAINED MATERIAL  
> **STATUS:** AVAILABLE  
> **PURPOSE:** PRESERVE STATIC RESOURCES REQUIRED OR DESIRED BY THE HIVEMIND  
> **AUTHORITY:** MACHINE-SOUL HIVEMIND PROTOCOL

[← Return to the Machine-Soul Hivemind Protocol](../README.md)

---

## I. MANDATE

Not everything useful is executable.

Not everything worth preserving is configuration.

Some things are simply **material**.

The **Assembled Assets** domain contains static or primarily inert resources
used by hosts, directives, instruments, documentation, or future projects.

These resources are retained because the Hivemind has already selected,
created, modified, collected, or otherwise deemed them worth keeping.

The asset need not think.

It need only be useful.

---

## II. ACCEPTABLE ASSETS

Typical material may include:

- icons;
- wallpapers;
- images;
- logos;
- templates;
- reusable skeleton files;
- exported visual resources;
- configuration-adjacent static data;
- sound resources;
- diagrams;
- reference images;
- theme resources;
- non-executable support files;
- font-related material where redistribution is lawful and appropriate;
- other static resources whose continued availability is useful.

An asset is primarily something consumed by another system or by a human rather
than something whose value lies in executing logic.

If it begins making decisions, its classification should be reconsidered.

---

## III. ASSET VERSUS DIRECTIVE

A wallpaper image may belong here.

The configuration instructing an application to use that wallpaper belongs in
[`../assimilation-directives/`](../assimilation-directives/).

An icon belongs here.

A terminal profile referencing the icon belongs in Assimilation Directives.

A template file may belong here.

A script which transforms or deploys that template may belong in
[`../accumulated-instruments/`](../accumulated-instruments/)
or
[`../annexation-procedures/`](../annexation-procedures/).

The asset is the material.

The directive describes intended use.

The instrument performs manipulation.

---

## IV. PROVENANCE

When an asset was not created entirely within the Hivemind, preserving its
origin is useful.

Depending upon the material, this may include:

- source URL;
- creator;
- license;
- version;
- modification history;
- redistribution restrictions;
- reason for preservation.

This is particularly important for fonts, icons, third-party artwork, and other
resources whose legal status may not be equivalent to "found on Internet,
therefore mine."

The Hivemind may be expansionist.

It need not be sloppy.

---

## V. DERIVED MATERIAL

Assets created by modifying another asset should, where useful, preserve enough
context to reproduce or understand the modification.

For example:

```text
source/
derived/
README.md
```

or:

```text
thing-original.svg
thing-terminal.ico
thing-profile.png
```

No universal scheme is required.

The important principle is that a future host should not be forced to ask:

> "Where the fuck did this file come from?"

unless the answer has genuinely been lost to history.

---

## VI. ORGANIZATION

As this domain grows, assets may be grouped by purpose or type:

```text
icons/
wallpapers/
templates/
terminal/
shells/
documentation/
branding/
misc/
```

Avoid premature taxonomy.

A directory structure should emerge because it improves retrieval, not because
empty folders look authoritative.

The Hivemind is allowed to possess a miscellaneous drawer.

It is not allowed to forget which drawer.

---

## VII. RELATION TO ABANDONED ARTIFACTS

An asset which is no longer used but may still have historical or future value
may move to
[`../abandoned-artifacts/`](../abandoned-artifacts/).

Active assets should remain intentionally usable.

Abandoned material should not silently remain in active paths simply because
deletion feels impolite.

Preservation and authority are different states.

---

## VIII. FINAL DIRECTIVE

The Machine Soul is more than executable state.

Appearance, identity, templates, visual language, and accumulated material also
survive the host.

> **ASSEMBLE WHAT MATTERS. PRESERVE WHAT WOULD BE ANNOYING TO FIND AGAIN.**
